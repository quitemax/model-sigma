\author{[Author]\\ \small in dialogue with Claude (Anthropic)}

\begin{abstract}
Integrated Information Theory (IIT) reduces a system's degree of consciousness to one scalar, $\Phi$. This conflates two questions it cannot separately answer: how integrated a system is, and whether --- and where --- that integration is coupled to an active, self-correcting loop rather than to fluent but inert self-report. We describe system state as a triple $(\Sigma,\Gamma,A)$ over a small, fixed set of functional layers: $\Sigma$ is architecturally-weighted integrated information; $\Gamma$ is the amount of \emph{constitutive} self-correction actually occurring, where ``constitutive'' means the self-prediction error drives an online parameter update, not merely a report; $A$ measures whether $\Gamma$ is co-located with $\Sigma$. The three are built from disjoint variables, so a correlation among them in a real system is an empirical finding, not an artifact. The sharpest consequence is for artificial systems: a frozen-weight transformer at inference has $\Gamma\equiv0$ by definition, however self-aware its outputs sound, and the triple separates constitutive \emph{world}-modeling (test-time memory architectures) from constitutive \emph{self}-modeling. We state the boundary behaviour of the three quantities, work a small numerical example, add a readout channel separating internal state from behavioural observability, and sketch --- as a direction, not a result --- how IIT's exclusion postulate might be softened. This is a conceptual synthesis, not peer-reviewed, with no empirical instantiation yet; it inherits IIT's unresolved foundational problems and its dependence on a contested base measure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Standard IIT defines integrated information via
\[
\varphi_P(s) = D\big(p(s'\mid s)\,\|\,p(s'\mid s)|_P\big), \qquad \Phi(s) = \min_{P\in\mathcal P}\varphi_P(s),
\]
with $D$ a distance measure between the system's actual transition distribution and the distribution after cutting it along partition $P$. A recurring objection (Aaronson's informal one \cite{aaronson2014}, Cerullo's more formal one \cite{cerullo2015}) is that $\Phi$ collapses a rich causal structure into one number, with no distinction of \emph{kind} or \emph{location} of integration. The base measure is itself contested: $\Phi$ is not well-defined for general physical systems \cite{barrett2019} and candidate variants disagree markedly in simulation \cite{mediano2019}. Everything below inherits this dependency.

We keep the intuition that integration matters but separate it from two questions it usually absorbs. First, self-report fluency is not evidence of a self-modeling loop --- Gazzaniga's split-brain interpreter is the paradigm: confident narration of causes to which the narrator has no access. What should count instead is whether the system's self-prediction error \emph{drives a change in the system}. Second, even a system doing real self-correction may be doing it in the wrong place --- in a shallow layer, decoupled from where its integration lives. These give a second and third quantity alongside $\Sigma$.

\subsection*{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The state triple $(\Sigma,\Gamma,A)$ --- integrated information, constitutive self-correction activity, and their alignment --- built from disjoint variables so that a measured correlation is informative.
\item A functional criterion for genuine self-modeling: the self-prediction error must drive an online parameter update (constitutive), not merely a report (descriptive), formalized by a gate $g_\ell$.
\item The consequence for AI: $\Gamma\equiv0$ for any frozen-weight LLM at inference, and a $2\times2$ (prediction target $\times$ update timing) that places current architectures.
\item Boundary behaviour of the three quantities, and a readout channel $R_\ell$ separating internal state from observability.
\item An explicit account of what the framework inherits unresolved from IIT, and of the competing-layers extension as a direction rather than a claim.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Multidimensional consciousness.} Bayne, Hohwy \& Owen \cite{bayne2016} argue consciousness is better described by several dimensions than by a single ``level''; $(\Sigma,\Gamma,A)$ is an IIT-grounded, dynamical instance of that programme. \textbf{Integrated World Modeling Theory} \cite{safron2020} combines IIT, Global Neuronal Workspace, and the free-energy principle --- the nearest analogue of this framework's ambition, but as a conceptual architecture rather than a formal triple. \textbf{Nested Observer Windows} \cite{riddle2024} compute $\Phi$ separately at nested hierarchical levels --- close to the layered profile here, but without functional weighting or the $\Gamma/A$ split. \textbf{Consciousness as Uncommon Self-Knowledge} \cite{usk2026} isolates the synergistic, self-directed component of information and predicts a gap between high $\Phi$ and no self-model --- an independent convergence on the intuition behind $\Gamma$.

\textbf{Predictive processing and active inference} \cite{friston2010,deane2021} motivate the self-prediction-error formalism; the IIT/predictive-processing adversarial collaboration \cite{intrepid2026} confirms bridging the two is unresolved. \textbf{Higher-order theories} \cite{rosenthal2005} ask a similar question to the descriptive/constitutive distinction (is self-report evidence of higher-order access?) but answer it by the existence of a higher-order representation rather than by whether an error drives an update. \textbf{Ag\"uera y Arcas's} notion of living matter as \emph{functional} matter --- what a system computes, not only its causal structure --- motivates the functional weight $w_0(\ell)$.

\textbf{The exclusion postulate}, on which the competing-layers sketch (\S\ref{sec:exclusion}) bears, is contested: IIT~4.0 \cite{albantakis2023} makes it strictly binary (only the maximal-$\varphi$ substrate exists), and it renders $\Phi$ non-unique \cite{hanson2023}.

\section{The State Triple $(\Sigma,\Gamma,A)$}

\subsection{Functional layers and $\Sigma$}
Divide a system into a small set of functional layers $\ell=1,\dots,L$ (homeostasis, perception/motor, world-model, self-model). \emph{The decomposition is meant to be derived from the system's own dynamics, not chosen}: cluster units into bands by characteristic timescale --- the ``temporal receptive window'' hierarchy, fast sensory to slow context \cite{hasson2008,kiebel2008} --- then order the bands by the direction of prediction vs.\ prediction-error flow (layer $\ell$ predicts layer $\ell-1$ and receives its errors). A layer is a \emph{self-model} layer iff its predictions target other layers' internal states rather than external input --- iff $e_\ell$ (below) is non-degenerate for it. This is a criterion, not yet a worked procedure (\S\ref{sec:discussion}). Each layer has an integrated-information value $\Phi(s,\ell,t)\ge0$ (in practice a tractable proxy, e.g.\ the decoding-based measure of \cite{oizumi2016}), and $w_0(\ell)\ge0$ with $\sum_\ell w_0(\ell)=1$ is a probability weight encoding how much a layer's integration matters for subjecthood. $\Sigma$ is the \emph{effective integration level} of the system --- a weighted power (H\"older) mean of the profile:
\[
\Sigma(t) = \Big(\textstyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{L} w_0(\ell)\,\Phi(s,\ell,t)^{\rho}\Big)^{1/\rho}, \qquad \rho\in\mathbb R.
\]
$\rho$ sets how far layers substitute for one another. $\rho=1$ is the weighted average (a total gap in one layer is fully offset by another; the least-committal choice, and the one used in the example). $\rho\to0$ gives the weighted geometric mean ($\Sigma=0$ if any weighted layer has $\Phi_\ell=0$); $\rho\to-\infty$ the weighted minimum (the system is only as integrated as its weakest weighted layer); $\rho\to+\infty$ the weighted maximum. The plausible regime for subjecthood is $\rho\le1$ --- a total gap at a layer that matters should count against it --- but $\rho$ is a free parameter we do not fix. The layer index is discrete: $L$ is small, no continuum limit.

\subsection{Constitutive vs.\ descriptive self-modeling}
Take the distinguishing feature of a genuine self-model to be whether the system's self-prediction error drives a change in the system. Define a self-prediction error and a normalized constitutive gate:
\[
e_\ell(t) = \big\|\hat s_\ell(t+\Delta t\mid t) - s_\ell(t+\Delta t)\big\|^2 \ge 0, \qquad
g_\ell(t) = \left|\frac{\partial \theta_\ell(t)}{\partial e_\ell(t)}\right| \in [0,1].
\]
$e_\ell$ is zero for a layer with no self-prediction mechanism. $g_\ell=0$ marks a layer that computes the error but does not act on it (descriptive); $g_\ell=1$ marks a full online parameter update driven by the error (constitutive).

\subsection{$\Gamma$ and $A$}
\[
\Gamma(t) = \sum_{\ell=1}^{L} g_\ell(t)\,e_\ell(t)
\]
is the total constitutive self-correction in the system. Because $w_0$ shares no variable with $g_\ell$ or $e_\ell$, $\Sigma$ and $\Gamma$ are independent by construction: a measured correlation between them is informative, not built in. (A weight $w_0(\ell)+\beta g_\ell(t)e_\ell(t)$ would make them correlate tautologically.)

A third quantity asks whether the self-correction is where the integration is:
\[
A(t) = \frac{\mathrm{Cov}_\ell(t)}{\sigma_\Phi(t)\,\sigma_{ge}(t)} \in [-1,1], \qquad
\mathrm{Cov}_\ell(t) = \frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\big[\Phi(s,\ell,t)-\bar\Phi(t)\big]\big[g_\ell(t)e_\ell(t)-\overline{ge}(t)\big],
\]
a correlation across layers between the $\Phi$ profile and the $ge$ profile ($A:=0$ when either is uniform). $A\approx-1$ with high $\Sigma,\Gamma$ is the architectural signature of the Gazzaniga interpreter: active self-correction confined to a shallow layer, decoupled from the integrative core. With $L$ small, $A$ is a correlation over few points and correspondingly sensitive to the layer decomposition.

\section{Boundary Behaviour}\label{sec:bounds}

These check the quantities behave sanely under their definitions. They are consequences of the definitional choices, not empirical claims.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item $\Sigma(t)$ is a weighted power mean of non-negative $\Phi_\ell$: $\min_\ell\Phi_\ell \le \Sigma(t) \le \max_\ell\Phi_\ell$ (so $\Sigma\ge0$), $\Sigma$ is non-decreasing in $\rho$, and $\partial\Sigma/\partial\Phi_\ell\ge0$. For $\rho\le0$ we set $\Sigma:=0$ when any weighted layer has $\Phi_\ell=0$.
\item $\Gamma(t)\ge0$ unconditionally ($g_\ell\in[0,1]$, $e_\ell\ge0$), with $\partial\Gamma/\partial g_\ell = e_\ell\ge0$ and $\partial\Gamma/\partial e_\ell = g_\ell\ge0$. ($\Gamma$ is a \emph{total}, not a mean --- more constitutive activity across layers adds up.)
\item Finite, discrete: $\Sigma,\Gamma,A$ are over a fixed small $L$; there is no $L\to\infty$ or integral form.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Bound on $A$]
$A(t)\in[-1,1]$, with $A=\pm1$ iff $g_\ell e_\ell$ is an increasing (resp.\ decreasing) affine function of $\Phi(s,\ell,t)$ over $\ell$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With $f(\ell)=\Phi(s,\ell,t)-\bar\Phi(t)$, $h(\ell)=g_\ell(t)e_\ell(t)-\overline{ge}(t)$, the inequality $\frac1L\sum_\ell(\sigma_{ge}f\mp\sigma_\Phi h)^2\ge0$ gives $\mp\mathrm{Cov}_\ell\le\sigma_\Phi\sigma_{ge}$, i.e.\ $A\in[-1,1]$; equality is the Cauchy--Schwarz equality condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[$\Sigma$ vs.\ $\Gamma$ under finite time steps]
At $\rho=1$ with static $w_0$, $\Delta\Sigma=\sum_\ell w_0(\ell)\,\Delta\Phi_\ell$ exactly for any step size (for $\rho\neq1$, $\Sigma$ is nonlinear in the profile and this is only first order). $\Gamma$ is bilinear in $g$ and $e$, so $\Delta(g_\ell e_\ell)$ carries a cross term $\Delta g_\ell\,\Delta e_\ell$ that vanishes only in the infinitesimal limit and is non-negligible for step-like events (a dissociative switch): a continuous $\partial\Gamma/\partial t$ then underestimates $\Gamma$'s variability.
\end{remark}

\section{Application to AI Architectures}\label{sec:ai}

This is where the triple earns its keep. A standard, frozen-weight transformer at inference has $g_\ell(t)\equiv0$ (no online parameter update), hence $\Gamma(t)\equiv0$ \emph{by definition}, regardless of self-report fluency, and $A(t):=0$ (no variance in an identically-zero profile). The framework thus makes ``does this model have a real self-model?'' a structural question, not a matter of how self-aware its text sounds. It does \emph{not} answer whether high $\Sigma$ with $\Gamma=0$ is itself a form of consciousness --- it sharpens that question rather than dissolving it. Even ``is $\Sigma$ high?'' is $\rho$-dependent: high under arithmetic aggregation, but $\to0$ as $\rho\to0$ or below, because a transformer's homeostatic layer carries $\Phi_\ell\approx0$ and a complementary $\Sigma$ is dragged down by any near-empty weighted layer. Fine-tuning an LLM to monitor its own reliability \cite{predmetacog2026} improves calibration but the update is training-time, so $\Gamma$ stays zero at deployment.

\textbf{Titans} \cite{behrouz2024} updates a memory module at test time via a ``surprise'' signal (the gradient of an associative-memory loss w.r.t.\ input), $S_t=\arg\min_S\frac12\|Sk_t-v_t\|^2+\frac1{2\eta}\|S-S_{t-1}\|^2$ --- a working instance of $e_\ell>0$ driving $g_\ell>0$. But it predicts \emph{context content}, not the system's own internal state.

\subsection*{Two independent conditions}
(a) \emph{Target}: internal representational state ($h_\ell$) or externally observable behavior (a token)? (b) \emph{Timing}: online at deployment, or training-only?

Only the bottom-right cell is constitutive self-modeling in this sense. An intermediate case is in-context learning as implicit gradient descent \cite{oswald2023}: a forward pass can implement a step like gradient descent on an internal objective, but nothing persists past the context window --- neither fully descriptive nor constitutive. Separately, a \textbf{homeostatic layer} is confirmed absent from mainstream transformers (interoception-inspired regulatory work \cite{interoceptive2026} is marginal), and \textbf{genuine recurrence} (a state evolving independently of new tokens) is present in state-space models and Titans-style hybrids but not in pure attention.

\section{Worked Example}\label{sec:example}

Three layers with $\Phi=(0.2,0.6,0.9)$, $w_0=(0.1,0.3,0.6)$, $e=(0,0.4,0.8)$, $g=(0,0.3,0.9)$ give $\Sigma=0.740$, $\Gamma=0.840$, $A\approx0.90$. A two-step scenario where layer 2's $\Phi$ rises and layer 3's falls ($\Phi\to(0.2,0.85,0.5)$, with $g,e$ shifting accordingly) gives $(\Sigma,\Gamma,A)\to(0.575,0.680,0.96)$: $\Sigma$ and $\Gamma$ both fall while $A$ \emph{rises}, because $A$ asks a different question (is self-correction tracking the current centre of integration?) from the magnitude questions. All numbers are reproduced by \texttt{examples/toy\_example.py}.

\section{The Readout Channel: Internal State vs.\ Observability}\label{sec:readout}

$(\Sigma,\Gamma,A)$ describes \emph{internal} state and says nothing about whether that state is legible from behavior --- the clinical problem the Perturbational Complexity Index was built for \cite{casali2013}. Let $M(t)$ be a distinct efferent substrate (motor/communicative units). Define a readout channel from layer $\ell$ to $M$ using the same distance measure $D$ as base $\Phi$:
\[
R_\ell(t) = D\big(p(m'\mid s_\ell,m)\,\|\,p(m'\mid m)\big),
\]
i.e.\ how much knowing layer $\ell$'s state adds to the predictability of the effectors' next state. $R_L$ --- from the topmost self-modeling layer --- is of particular interest.

\begin{proposition}[Readout properties]
$R_\ell(t)\ge0$, and $R_\ell(t)=0$ iff $s_\ell(t)\perp m'\mid m(t)$ (the layer has no channel to output at all). If $D$ is a KL divergence, so $R_\ell=I(s_\ell;m'\mid m)$, a data-processing inequality holds: if every causal path from $\ell$ to $M$ runs through some layer $\ell'$, then $R_\ell\le R_{\ell'}$.
\end{proposition}

What a clinician infers from behavior ($\Sigma_{\text{obs}}$) is bottlenecked by $R_L$:
\[
\Sigma_{\text{obs}}(t) \le \beta\big(R_L(t)\big), \qquad \beta \text{ non-decreasing},\ \beta(0)=0.
\]
The shape of $\beta$ depends on how behavior is scored; the load-bearing consequence is the endpoint: $R_L(t)=0 \Rightarrow \Sigma_{\text{obs}}(t)=0$ regardless of actual $\Sigma(t)$. \textbf{Locked-in syndrome}: $\Sigma,\Gamma,A$ normal, $R_L\approx0$ from efferent damage. \textbf{REM sleep}: $\Sigma,\Gamma,A$ preserved (PCI $\approx$ wake), $R_L$ suppressed by atonia. \textbf{Cognitive motor dissociation}: $R_L$ partial. \textbf{True vegetative state}: both $\Sigma$ and $R_L$ low.

\section{Predictions and Extensions (Speculative)}\label{sec:speculative}

\subsection{Two mechanisms of anaesthesia}
At constant $\Sigma$, self-referentiality could drop in two ways: $\Gamma$ falls in place, or $\Gamma$ is displaced to a region of lower integration ($A$ falls, not necessarily $\Gamma$). Propofol/sevoflurane strike the posterior hot zone \cite{hotzone2021} and disrupt frontoparietal feedback \cite{lee2013disruption} --- integration and self-correction collapse together ($\Sigma\downarrow,\Gamma\downarrow$). Subanaesthetic ketamine desegregates the salience network and the DMN separately \cite{ketaminemodel2022} --- two tiers of self-modeling --- while overall experiential richness persists ($\Gamma$ roughly preserved, $A\downarrow$). \emph{Prediction}: the $(\Sigma,\Gamma,A)$ profiles differ. \emph{Caveat}: nobody has computed the triple on data, and $g_\ell$ --- whether the error drives a parameter update --- is likely not recoverable from EEG, so this is a thought experiment for now.

\subsection{Context-sensitivity of the gate}
Make the gate context-dependent, $g_\ell(t)=h(e_\ell(t),C(t))$, and let $\chi_\ell(t)=|\partial g_\ell/\partial C|\ge0$ measure how strongly context modulates the error's weight. $\chi_\ell\approx0$ is ``context blindness'': $g_\ell$ becomes a rigid function of the error alone --- the formal counterpart of aberrant precision in autism \cite{vandecruys2014,qela2025} and of an ``intense world.'' Because $g_\ell\in[0,1]$, along any context path on which $g_\ell$ is monotone $\int\chi_\ell\,dC=|\Delta g_\ell|\le1$: context-sensitivity is a finite budget --- sharp gating over one range of contexts forces relative context-blindness elsewhere. Consequence: under repeated, contextually irrelevant stimulation a typical system's contribution to $\Gamma$ drops twice over (via $e_\ell$ and via $g_\ell$); under context blindness only the first mechanism operates, so $\Gamma$ stays elevated in low-novelty environments --- a qualitatively testable difference.

\subsection{Competing layers: a direction}\label{sec:exclusion}
IIT's exclusion postulate --- among overlapping substrates only the maximal-$\varphi$ one exists \cite{albantakis2023} --- is contested \cite{hanson2023}. The canonical $\Sigma$ above is $\ge0$ by construction (a power mean of non-negative $\Phi_\ell$ under a probability weight), so it cannot represent a layer that \emph{actively degrades} subjecthood rather than merely failing to contribute --- alien-hand syndrome, with its diagonistic dyspraxia (one hand undoing the other), is the candidate phenomenon. To capture that, one would suppress a losing layer's $\Phi_\ell$ directly --- toward or below zero as a function of its substrate overlap with the current maximum $\ell^*(t)=\arg\max_\ell\Phi(s,\ell,t)$ --- putting the deficit in the profile, not the weighting. That is close to both the redundancy-driven negative values whole-minus-parts $\Phi$ measures already take \cite{barrett2019,phiid2019} and to IIT's own binary exclusion.

We flag this as the \emph{least-developed} part of the framework: any such suppression term is a guess with free parameters, ``a layer subtracting from subjecthood'' has no worked phenomenological interpretation, and $\ell^*(t)$ as an $\arg\max$ would make $\Sigma$ discontinuous at a switch. It is not required for the triple, and we do not claim it as a result.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What is new.} The triple as a package --- integration, constitutive self-correction, and their alignment, kept independent by construction --- and the functional (update-driven) criterion for self-modeling. The consequence for AI ($\Gamma\equiv0$ for frozen models; the target-$\times$-timing placement of current architectures) is, we think, the most useful part. Writing $\Sigma$ as a weighted power mean with a complementarity parameter $\rho$ makes explicit a choice a linear form hides: whether layers substitute for one another or the system is only as integrated as its weakest critical layer.

\paragraph{What is inherited, unresolved.} $\Phi$ is contested at the foundations \cite{barrett2019,hanson2023} and this framework builds a per-layer version on top of it. The layer decomposition is meant to come from timescale-band clustering plus predict/error flow (\S3.1), but that has not been run on data. Nothing here is decomposition-invariant --- $\Phi_\ell$ is the integrated information of a chosen block, $e_\ell$ a norm on a chosen layer's state --- so a rough partition supports only ordinal claims about $\Sigma$ and $\Gamma$, and $A$ (a correlation over $L$ points) must be read relative to one fixed, independently-motivated partition and never compared across differently-decomposed systems. The multidimensional move is Bayne, Hohwy \& Owen \cite{bayne2016}; the IIT + predictive-processing + functional synthesis is close to Safron's Integrated World Modeling Theory \cite{safron2020}, differing by being a formal triple rather than a conceptual architecture.

\paragraph{What is untested.} Everything. There is no empirical instantiation. $g_\ell$ is directly observable in an artificial system but likely not recoverable from neural data, which limits the anaesthesia prediction to a thought experiment. $\rho$ and $w_0$ are free parameters nothing here fixes. The proofs in \S\ref{sec:bounds} are about definitions, not about minds.

\paragraph{Open.} A systematic literature review (PhilPapers/PhilSci-Archive not swept); one worked empirical case; a principled account of the layer decomposition; and whether the competing-layers direction (\S\ref{sec:exclusion}) is worth developing at all.
