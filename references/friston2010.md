# friston2010

**Friston, K.** (2010). *The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory?* Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 11(2), 127–138.

- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2787
- Type: journal article (review)
- Open access: no (the summary below is not verbatim)

## Summary (not verbatim)
The free-energy principle (FEP) holds that any self-organizing system at equilibrium with its
environment must minimize its variational free energy — an upper bound on the "surprise"
(negative log-likelihood) of sensory samples given a generative model. The paper shows how
perception, learning and action (active inference) can be cast as minimization of the same
quantity, and unifies predictive coding, Bayesian inference, optimal control theory and
reinforcement learning. Key notion: **precision** — the weight (inverse variance) assigned to
prediction errors, modulated by context.

## Role in Model Σ
The predictive-processing / active-inference framework from which the model borrows the
*self-prediction error* e_ℓ(t) and the constitutivity criterion (online updates driven by that
error). In §18, FEP is the source of the notion of **precision** — "the weight given to
expectations" is structurally exactly the gate g_ℓ(t); the parameter χ_ℓ = |∂g_ℓ/∂C| closes
the gap: how context modulates that weight.
